Swap Platform Domain Glossary
1. Core Product Concepts
Swap Platform

A platform that allows users to exchange one asset for another. The assets may be fiat currencies, cryptocurrencies, stablecoins, tokens, or other digital assets.

Related terms: swap, quote, transaction, liquidity provider, on-ramp, off-ramp.

Swap

A business operation where a user exchanges one asset for another, for example USD to USDT, BTC to ETH, or USDT to EUR.

A swap usually includes a source asset, target asset, amount, exchange rate, fees, and settlement details.

Example:
A user swaps 100 USDT into ETH.

On-ramp

A flow where a user buys crypto using fiat money.

Example:
User pays with a bank card and receives USDT in their wallet.

Related terms: fiat-to-crypto, card payment, invoice, KYC.

Off-ramp

A flow where a user sells crypto and receives fiat money.

Example:
User sends USDT and receives EUR to their bank card or bank account.

Related terms: crypto-to-fiat, payout, withdrawal, bank transfer.

Crypto-to-Crypto Swap

A swap where both the source and target assets are cryptocurrencies or tokens.

Example:
USDT → ETH.

Fiat-to-Crypto Swap

A swap where the user pays fiat currency and receives crypto.

Example:
EUR → BTC.

Crypto-to-Fiat Swap

A swap where the user sends crypto and receives fiat currency.

Example:
USDT → UAH.

Asset

Any unit of value supported by the platform.

Assets may include fiat currencies, native blockchain coins, stablecoins, ERC-20 tokens, SPL tokens, or internal platform balances.

Examples:
USD, EUR, UAH, BTC, ETH, USDT, USDC.

Currency

A fiat asset issued by a state or central bank.

Examples:
USD, EUR, UAH, GBP.

Coin

A native asset of a blockchain network.

Examples:
BTC on Bitcoin, ETH on Ethereum, SOL on Solana.

Token

A digital asset issued on top of a blockchain network, usually through a smart contract.

Examples:
USDT on Ethereum, USDC on Polygon, DAI on Arbitrum.

Important:
The word “token” is ambiguous. It may mean a crypto token, a card token, an API access token, or a session token depending on context.

2. Transaction and Business Entities
Transaction

A high-level business record that represents a value movement or an exchange operation inside the platform.

A transaction may include payment, swap, payout, refund, blockchain transfer, provider interaction, and status history.

Common fields:
id, userId, type, sourceAsset, targetAsset, amount, status, provider, createdAt, updatedAt.

Related terms: order, invoice, payment, payout, blockchain transaction.

Order

A user’s request to execute a swap or payment operation.

An order usually represents the user intent, while a transaction represents the actual processing lifecycle.

Example:
The user creates an order to buy 500 USDT using EUR.

Invoice

A payment request generated by the platform or payment provider.

An invoice contains the amount to be paid, currency, payment method, expiration time, payment address or URL, and current status.

Example:
An invoice is created for 100 EUR and expires in 15 minutes.

Related terms: payment link, payment request, checkout session, TTL.

Payment

An incoming money movement from the user to the platform, merchant, or provider.

Examples:
Card payment, bank transfer, crypto deposit.

Payout

An outgoing money movement from the platform, merchant, or provider to the user.

Examples:
Card withdrawal, bank transfer payout, crypto withdrawal.

Deposit

An incoming transfer of funds to the platform or user account.

In Web3, a deposit usually means the user sends crypto to a generated deposit address.

Withdrawal

An outgoing transfer of funds from the platform to a user-controlled destination.

In Web3, a withdrawal usually means sending crypto to the user’s wallet address.

Refund

A reversal of a completed or partially completed payment.

A refund returns funds to the original payment source.

Reversal

A cancellation or rollback of a payment before final settlement.

In card payments, a reversal often happens before the transaction is fully captured or settled.

Chargeback

A dispute initiated by the cardholder through the issuing bank.

Chargebacks are common in card payments and may lead to financial loss, fraud investigation, or account restrictions.

Settlement

The final movement of money between financial institutions, providers, merchants, or liquidity partners.

A transaction may be authorized before it is settled.

Reconciliation

The process of comparing internal transaction records with provider reports, blockchain data, bank statements, or ledger entries.

The goal is to detect mismatches, missing payments, duplicated payouts, or incorrect balances.

Ledger

An internal accounting system that records balance changes.

A ledger should usually be append-only and auditable.

Related terms: balance, debit, credit, journal entry.

Balance

The amount of funds available or recorded for a user, merchant, wallet, or platform account.

3. Quote, Rate, and Pricing
Quote

A temporary offer that defines how much of the target asset the user will receive for a given source amount.

A quote usually has a TTL and may expire if not accepted in time.

Example:
Pay 1000 USDT and receive 0.32 ETH.

Exchange Rate

The price ratio between two assets.

Example:
1 ETH = 3200 USDT.

Rate Lock

A mechanism that guarantees a quoted exchange rate for a limited period.

Rate locks protect the user from market changes during payment.

Floating Rate

A rate model where the final received amount may change depending on market price at the time of execution.

Fixed Rate

A rate model where the target amount is guaranteed if the user completes the payment before expiration.

Spread

The difference between the market price and the price offered to the user.

Spread may be used as a revenue source or risk buffer.

Fee

An amount charged for processing a transaction.

Fees may include platform fee, provider fee, blockchain network fee, liquidity provider fee, or card processing fee.

Network Fee

A fee paid to a blockchain network to process a transaction.

Related terms: gas fee, miner fee, validator fee.

Gas Fee

A blockchain transaction fee, especially common in EVM-compatible networks.

Gas is paid to execute transactions or smart contract calls.

Slippage

The difference between the expected execution price and the actual execution price.

Slippage usually happens because of market movement or low liquidity.

Minimum Amount

The smallest amount allowed for a transaction or swap.

Maximum Amount

The largest amount allowed for a transaction or swap.

TTL

Time to live. The period during which an invoice, quote, token, session, or payment link remains valid.

Example:
A checkout invoice has a TTL of 15 minutes.

4. Payment Integration Flows
H2H

Host-to-host integration.

A server-to-server integration where the platform backend communicates directly with the provider backend.

In card payments, H2H may increase PCI scope if sensitive cardholder data touches the platform infrastructure.

Related terms: API integration, server-side payment, direct integration.

Redirect

A payment flow where the user is redirected from the platform to a provider-hosted page.

The provider collects payment details, processes the payment, and redirects the user back to the platform.

Related terms: hosted payment page, return URL, callback URL.

Iframe

A payment flow where the provider’s payment form is embedded into the platform UI.

The user stays visually inside the platform, but sensitive payment data may be handled by the provider.

Related terms: embedded checkout, hosted fields, widget.

Hosted Payment Page

A provider-hosted checkout page where the user enters payment details.

This reduces the platform’s exposure to sensitive payment data.

Aliases: HPP, hosted checkout.

SDK

Software Development Kit.

A provider-supplied library used to integrate payment, wallet, checkout, KYC, or Web3 functionality.

Example:
A card provider SDK renders secure card fields in the frontend.

Widget

An embeddable UI component provided by a third party.

Widgets are common for checkout, KYC, wallet connection, crypto purchase, or fiat on-ramp flows.

Payment Link

A URL that opens a payment page for a specific invoice or checkout session.

Return URL

The URL where the user is redirected after completing or cancelling a payment.

Callback URL

A backend endpoint used by a provider to notify the platform about transaction status changes.

Related terms: webhook, notification URL, IPN.

Webhook

An HTTP callback sent by a provider to notify the platform about an event.

Examples:
Payment completed, payment failed, payout cancelled, deposit confirmed.

P2P

Peer-to-peer.

Depending on context, P2P may mean:

A direct user-to-user exchange model.
A card-to-card transfer flow.
A marketplace where users trade with each other while the platform provides escrow and dispute resolution.

The exact meaning should be defined per platform.

APM

Alternative Payment Method.

Any payment method other than traditional card payments.

Examples:
Bank transfer, PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay, iDEAL, Bancontact, Blik, SEPA, local wallets.

5. Card Payments and PCI
Card

A payment instrument issued by a bank or financial institution.

Cards may be debit, credit, prepaid, virtual, or corporate.

PAN

Primary Account Number.

The full card number printed on the card.

PAN is sensitive cardholder data and must be protected.

BIN / IIN

Bank Identification Number or Issuer Identification Number.

The first digits of a card number that identify the issuing bank, card network, country, and card type.

Expiry Date

The month and year when a card expires.

CVV / CVC

The card verification code used to prove that the user has access to the physical or virtual card.

CVV/CVC must not be stored after authorization.

Cardholder Data

Sensitive data related to a payment card.

Usually includes PAN, cardholder name, expiry date, and service code.

PCI DSS

Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard.

A security standard for organizations that store, process, or transmit cardholder data.

PCI Scope

The part of the system, infrastructure, people, and processes that are subject to PCI DSS requirements.

A redirect or iframe integration may reduce PCI scope. H2H card processing usually increases PCI scope.

Tokenization

The process of replacing sensitive payment data with a non-sensitive token.

The token can be reused for future payments without exposing the original card data.

Card Token

A provider-generated token that represents a card.

A card token is not the same as a crypto token or API token.

Network Token

A token issued by a card network, such as Visa or Mastercard, to represent a card in a safer way.

3DS

3-D Secure.

An authentication protocol used to verify the cardholder during online card payments.

Related terms: 3DS1, 3DS2, challenge flow, frictionless flow.

ACS

Access Control Server.

The issuer-side system that authenticates the cardholder during 3DS.

ECI

Electronic Commerce Indicator.

A value that indicates the result and type of 3DS authentication.

Authorization

A request to reserve funds on the cardholder’s account.

Authorization does not always mean that the funds are finally settled.

Capture

The operation that confirms an authorized payment and moves it toward settlement.

Void

The cancellation of an authorization before capture or settlement.

Decline

A failed payment authorization or processing attempt.

Declines may happen because of insufficient funds, fraud rules, issuer rejection, invalid card data, 3DS failure, or provider restrictions.

6. Web3 and Blockchain Concepts
Blockchain

A distributed ledger where transactions are grouped into blocks and verified by network participants.

Network / Chain

A specific blockchain environment.

Examples:
Bitcoin, Ethereum, Tron, Solana, Polygon, Arbitrum, BNB Smart Chain.

Mainnet

The production blockchain network where real assets have value.

Testnet

A testing blockchain network used for development and QA.

Assets on testnets usually have no real-world value.

Wallet

A tool or account used to manage blockchain addresses and sign transactions.

A wallet may be custodial or non-custodial.

Address

A public blockchain identifier used to send or receive assets.

Example:
An Ethereum address starts with 0x.

Private Key

A secret cryptographic key used to sign blockchain transactions.

Anyone with the private key can control the assets.

Public Key

A cryptographic key derived from the private key and used to verify signatures.

Seed Phrase

A human-readable backup phrase used to restore a wallet.

A seed phrase must never be shared with anyone.

Custodial Wallet

A wallet where the platform or third party controls the private keys on behalf of the user.

Non-custodial Wallet

A wallet where the user controls their own private keys.

The platform cannot move funds without the user’s signature.

Smart Contract

A program deployed on a blockchain.

Smart contracts can hold funds, execute swaps, manage escrow, or interact with DeFi protocols.

EOA

Externally Owned Account.

A blockchain account controlled by a private key, not by smart contract code.

Smart Contract Wallet

A wallet implemented as a smart contract.

It may support advanced features such as multisig, account recovery, spending limits, or batching.

Transaction Hash

A unique identifier of a blockchain transaction.

Aliases: tx hash, txid.

Blockchain Transaction

A transaction submitted to a blockchain network.

It may transfer assets, call a smart contract, approve token spending, or deploy a contract.

Confirmation

A signal that a blockchain transaction has been included in a block and accepted by the network.

More confirmations usually mean lower rollback risk.

Finality

The point at which a blockchain transaction is considered irreversible or practically irreversible.

Mempool

A waiting area for blockchain transactions that have been broadcast but not yet included in a block.

Nonce

A sequence number used to order transactions from the same blockchain account.

Incorrect nonce handling may cause stuck, replaced, or failed transactions.

Gas Limit

The maximum amount of gas a user is willing to spend on a blockchain transaction.

Gas Price

The price paid per unit of gas.

Native Coin

The main coin used to pay network fees on a blockchain.

Examples:
ETH on Ethereum, MATIC on Polygon, TRX on Tron, SOL on Solana.

Stablecoin

A token designed to maintain a stable value relative to a fiat currency.

Examples:
USDT, USDC, DAI.

Token Standard

A technical specification that defines how tokens behave on a blockchain.

Examples:
ERC-20, ERC-721, ERC-1155, SPL, TRC-20.

Approval

A blockchain operation where a user allows a smart contract to spend tokens on their behalf.

Common in ERC-20 token flows.

Allowance

The amount of tokens that a smart contract is allowed to spend from a user’s wallet.

Bridge

A system that moves assets or messages between blockchain networks.

Bridges may introduce security, liquidity, and finality risks.

DEX

Decentralized Exchange.

A blockchain-based exchange where users trade assets through smart contracts.

AMM

Automated Market Maker.

A type of DEX that uses liquidity pools instead of traditional order books.

Liquidity Pool

A pool of assets locked in a smart contract and used for swaps.

Liquidity Provider

An entity or user that provides assets to support swaps.

In a centralized platform, a liquidity provider may also be a third-party company or exchange partner.

7. Statuses and Lifecycle
Created

The transaction, order, or invoice has been created but not yet processed.

Pending

The transaction is waiting for payment, confirmation, provider response, or blockchain finality.

Processing

The platform or provider is actively processing the transaction.

Authorized

The payment has been approved, but funds may not yet be captured or settled.

Completed

The transaction finished successfully.

Failed

The transaction could not be completed.

Cancelled

The transaction was stopped before completion.

Cancellation may be initiated by the user, platform, provider, timeout, or risk system.

Expired

The invoice, quote, or payment session is no longer valid because its TTL has passed.

Rejected

The transaction was refused by the platform, provider, risk engine, compliance system, or blockchain validation logic.

Refunded

Funds were returned to the original payer.

Partially Completed

Only part of the expected flow was completed.

Example:
The user paid crypto, but fiat payout is still pending.

Stuck

A transaction is not progressing because of provider delay, blockchain congestion, nonce issue, insufficient gas, or missing callback.

8. Risk, Compliance, and Security
KYC

Know Your Customer.

A process used to verify the identity of a user.

KYB

Know Your Business.

A process used to verify a business customer or merchant.

AML

Anti-Money Laundering.

A set of controls used to detect and prevent money laundering.

Sanctions Screening

A compliance check that verifies whether a user, wallet, entity, or country is restricted.

Travel Rule

A regulatory requirement that may require sharing sender and receiver information for certain crypto transfers.

Fraud Check

A risk assessment that detects suspicious activity.

Signals may include device fingerprint, IP address, card country, velocity, user history, blockchain risk, or behavioral patterns.

Velocity Limit

A limit based on transaction frequency or total volume over time.

Example:
Maximum 5 card payments per user per hour.

Risk Score

A numeric or categorical score that represents the risk level of a user, transaction, wallet, or payment method.

Blacklist

A list of blocked users, cards, wallets, IPs, countries, or devices.

Whitelist

A list of trusted or explicitly allowed users, wallets, IPs, or partners.

Escrow

A mechanism where funds are held temporarily until required conditions are met.

Common in P2P flows and dispute resolution.

Dispute

A conflict between parties about a transaction.

Disputes may involve failed delivery, chargeback, underpayment, wrong address, fraud, or P2P disagreement.

9. API and Integration Terms
API

Application Programming Interface.

A contract that allows systems to communicate with each other.

API Key

A secret credential used to authenticate API requests.

Access Token

A temporary credential used to access protected API resources.

Not the same as a crypto token or card token.

Idempotency Key

A unique key used to safely retry requests without creating duplicate operations.

Example:
Creating the same payout twice with the same idempotency key should not create two payouts.

Signature

A cryptographic value used to verify that a request, webhook, or blockchain transaction is authentic.

Webhook Signature

A signature sent with a webhook to prove that the event came from the expected provider.

Correlation ID

An identifier used to trace a request across multiple services.

Provider Reference

An external ID assigned by a payment provider, liquidity provider, bank, or blockchain service.

Internal Reference

An ID generated by the platform to track a transaction internally.

Callback Retry

A repeated attempt to deliver a webhook or callback after a failure.

Timeout

A maximum waiting period for an operation.

Timeouts are common for provider requests, payment sessions, blockchain confirmations, and quote validity.

10. Provider and Infrastructure Concepts
Payment Provider

A third-party company that processes payments or payouts.

Aliases: PSP, acquirer, processor, payment gateway.

Payment Gateway

A service that connects the platform to payment methods, acquirers, card networks, or banks.

Acquirer

A financial institution that processes card payments on behalf of a merchant.

Issuer

The bank or institution that issued the user’s card.

Card Network

A network that routes card transactions between issuers and acquirers.

Examples:
Visa, Mastercard.

Liquidity Provider

A partner that provides asset liquidity and pricing for swaps.

Custody Provider

A third-party service that stores and manages crypto assets on behalf of the platform or users.

Blockchain Node

A server that connects to a blockchain network and provides access to blockchain data and transaction broadcasting.

RPC Provider

A service that exposes blockchain node access through RPC APIs.

Examples:
Used to read balances, estimate gas, send transactions, or fetch transaction receipts.

Indexer

A service that reads blockchain data and makes it searchable or easier to query.

Oracle

A service that provides external data to smart contracts or blockchain systems.

Example:
Price oracle for ETH/USD.

11. Common Ambiguous Terms
Token

May refer to:

Crypto token — an asset on a blockchain.
Card token — a replacement for sensitive card data.
Access token — API authentication credential.
Session token — temporary frontend or backend session identifier.

The meaning should be inferred from context.

Transaction

May refer to:

Business transaction inside the platform.
Payment provider transaction.
Blockchain transaction.
Database transaction.

The system should disambiguate based on fields such as txHash, providerReference, status, asset, or paymentMethod.

Address

May refer to:

Blockchain wallet address.
User billing address.
IP address.
Smart contract address.
Network

May refer to:

Blockchain network.
Card network.
Computer network.
Payment network.
Provider

May refer to:

Payment provider.
Liquidity provider.
Custody provider.
RPC provider.
KYC provider.
12. Suggested Entity Relationships
User creates Order
Order generates Quote
Quote may generate Invoice
Invoice receives Payment
Payment updates Transaction
Transaction may trigger Swap
Swap uses Liquidity Provider
Swap may create Blockchain Transaction
Blockchain Transaction has Transaction Hash
Transaction may end with Payout
Payout may use Payment Provider
Provider sends Webhook
Webhook updates Transaction Status
Ledger records Balance Changes
